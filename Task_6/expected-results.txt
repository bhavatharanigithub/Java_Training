DAY 8 - SET 2: INSURANCE POLICY REPORTING
Expected Results and Join Explanations (MySQL 8.4)

The SQL script creates a separate database named insurance_reporting_db.
Its sample policy dates use CURDATE(), so expiry and monthly reports adapt
to the date on which you run the script. Ages are also calculated as of today.

1. POLICIES EXPIRING IN THE NEXT 30 DAYS
Expected sample policies:
- 1001 - Ananya Kumar - Health Insurance - expires in 10 days.
- 1004 - Meera Iyer - Health Insurance - expires in 25 days.
The output also contains masked policy numbers and days_until_expiry.
INNER JOIN is appropriate because a policy must have a matching customer
and product to appear in this detailed report. DATE_ADD creates the 30-day
cutoff; DATEDIFF calculates days remaining. SUBSTRING and CONCAT mask IDs.

2. PREMIUM PER PRODUCT
Expected aggregate values:

Product              Policies   Total annual premium
Health Insurance        3          36,000.00
Motor Insurance         3          28,000.00
Life Insurance          2          35,000.00
Travel Insurance        2           9,000.00

The query also returns average annual premium. INNER JOIN is appropriate
because the report lists products that have at least one policy.

3. CUSTOMERS WITH MORE THAN ONE ACTIVE POLICY
Expected customers:
- Ananya Kumar (101): 2 active policies; premium total 20,000.00.
- Rahul Sharma (102): 2 active policies; premium total 34,000.00.
- Divya Nair (105): 2 active policies; premium total 16,000.00.
INNER JOIN finds customers with policies. WHERE filters active, unexpired
policies. GROUP BY creates customer groups and HAVING keeps counts above one.

4. CLAIM RATIO PER PRODUCT
Definition used in this exercise:
    Claim ratio (%) = total claim amount / total annual premium * 100

Expected values:

Product              Premium total   Claim total   Claim ratio
Health Insurance       36,000.00      16,500.00       45.83%
Motor Insurance        28,000.00       5,000.00       17.86%
Life Insurance         35,000.00      15,000.00       42.86%
Travel Insurance        9,000.00           0.00        0.00%

Claims are first aggregated by policy in a CTE. This prevents multiple claim
rows for one policy from multiplying its premium in the final sum.
INNER JOIN links policies to products. LEFT JOIN retains policies without
claims and COALESCE treats their claim amount as zero.

5. POLICIES WITHOUT CLAIMS
Expected policy IDs: 1006, 1007, 1009 and 1010.
LEFT JOIN keeps every policy even if there is no matching claim. The
WHERE claim_id IS NULL condition returns only policies without claims.
INNER JOINs to customers and products supply readable names.

6. AVERAGE PREMIUM BY AGE BAND
The query returns these four bands:
- Under 25
- 25-39
- 40-59
- 60+
It reports customer_count, policy_count and average_annual_premium for
active policies. Exact age membership depends on the execution date.
CROSS JOIN creates every age-band/customer combination. LEFT JOIN attaches
matching active policies and preserves bands with no matching policy.
An empty band's average is NULL.

7. MONTHLY NEW POLICIES
The result contains one row for each product and the number of policies
whose start_date falls in the current calendar month. Policy 1007 starts
today, so Life Insurance has at least one new policy. The exact counts are
calculated when you run the script.
LEFT JOIN ensures products with zero new policies still appear. The date
range includes the first day of this month and excludes the first day of
the next month.

8. TOP FIVE AGENTS BY PREMIUM
Expected ranking from the sample data:
1. Priya Menon (202): 38,000.00
2. Sanjay Patel (201): 34,000.00
3. Vikram Singh (203): 25,000.00
4. Nisha Reddy (204): 16,000.00
There are four sample agents, so LIMIT 5 returns four rows. INNER JOIN
includes agents who have at least one policy.

EXTRA: PRODUCTS WITH CLAIM RATIO ABOVE 60%
Expected result: no rows for the supplied sample data, because none of the
four product ratios exceeds 60%. GROUP BY calculates one ratio per product;
HAVING filters the grouped results after aggregation.

JOIN CHOICE SUMMARY
- INNER JOIN: Queries 1, 2, 3 and 8. Use when matching related records are
  required for the report.
- LEFT JOIN: Queries 4, 5 and 7. Use when rows from the main table must
  remain even if a related claim or monthly policy is missing.
- CROSS JOIN: Query 6. Use to generate all age-band/customer combinations
  before matching policies to age bands.

FUNCTIONS
- DATE_ADD: adds 30 days to the current date for the expiry window.
- DATEDIFF: returns the number of days between today and policy expiry.
- CASE: classifies ages into readable age bands.
- CONCAT: joins visible and masked parts of a policy number.
- SUBSTRING: extracts selected characters from the policy number.

IMPORTANT BUSINESS DEFINITION
This exercise uses total claim amount divided by total annual premium as a
simple claim ratio. A real insurer may use incurred claims divided by earned
premium for a defined reporting period, and may include outstanding reserves.
Confirm the insurer's formal definition before using this in production.

HOW TO RUN
1. Open MySQL Workbench and connect to MySQL 8.4.
2. Open reports.sql and execute the complete script.
3. Review each result grid after its labelled query.
4. Compare the results with this document.

The data is synthetic and intended for SQL practice only.
